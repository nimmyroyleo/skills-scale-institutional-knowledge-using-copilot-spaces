# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a structured, customer-first project management approach that
combines clear ownership, iterative delivery, data-informed decisions, and
continuous improvement. These documents provide a central framework for
delivering product features, services, and integrations consistently.

## Project Management Processes

OctoAcme manages work through five lifecycle phases: initiation, planning,
execution, release, and close and retrospective. Teams validate the business
need, success measures, stakeholders, and initial risks before moving from
initiation to planning. Planning turns approved work into a prioritized,
estimated backlog of shippable increments with acceptance criteria, a
Definition of Done, milestones, dependencies, and a release plan. Execution
then combines build, test, review, and iteration while the project board
provides a single source of truth for delivery status.

Project Managers coordinate delivery schedules, risks, dependencies, and
cross-team communication. Product Managers define the customer and business
outcomes, prioritize the roadmap and backlog, and measure success. Developers
design, implement, test, estimate, and review work; QA and testing roles
validate acceptance criteria and quality; and stakeholders provide inputs and
approvals. This shared ownership gives each project clear accountability while
keeping product, delivery, and technical decisions aligned.

The communication cadence keeps progress and decisions visible: delivery teams
hold regular standups to surface progress, blockers, and dependencies; PM and
Product Manager syncs align priorities and risks; and stakeholder updates occur
weekly or at milestones, with broader monthly reporting where appropriate.
Risk registers are reviewed weekly, and escalations progress from the delivery
team to the Project Manager, Product Lead, and Sponsor when necessary. Status
updates and release documentation serve as accessible, shared sources of
project information.

Quality is built into delivery through clear acceptance criteria, a documented
Definition of Done, small pull requests, peer review, automated tests and
linting, and CI security scanning. Teams add unit and integration tests as
applicable, run end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, and perform manual
QA when feature acceptance requires it. Releases require completed acceptance
criteria, passing checks, pre-release verification, release notes, and a
rollback plan. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, milestones, and
incidents turn lessons into two or three owned follow-up actions, reinforcing
continuous improvement and helping new team members onboard quickly.
